For years my mornings began the same way: an alarm, a thumb, a glowing rectangle full of other people's urgency. By the time the coffee was ready I had already answered three messages and absorbed two arguments I wasn't part of.

So I tried something small. For thirty days, the phone stayed in another room until I had finished a single cup of tea by the window.

## What changed

- **The day felt longer.** Not because it was, but because the first hour belonged to me.
- **I noticed the light.** October sunrise is a slow, amber thing. I had been missing it.
- **Work got easier.** Arriving at my inbox rested, rather than ambushed, made every reply calmer.

> The point was never productivity. The point was to remember that the morning existed before the world asked anything of it.

I still check my phone. I just do it second.
