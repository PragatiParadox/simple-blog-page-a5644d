I have lived in the same neighbourhood for six years and, until last month, I had walked roughly four of its streets. Home to the station. Station to the market. Market to home.

On a grey Saturday I left the house with one rule: at every corner, turn toward whatever looks most interesting.

## What I found

1. A tiny bookshop run out of someone's front room, open "most Saturdays, weather permitting."
2. A lane of terraced houses, each door painted a different shade of green.
3. A community garden where a man in a yellow raincoat offered me a fig.

None of these were more than ten minutes from my front door. I had simply never had a reason to pass them.

> A destination tells you where to look. Without one, you have to look everywhere.

I got home soaked, three hours later, with a secondhand poetry collection and a strong urge to do it again.
